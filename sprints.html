Sprint Planning

Capacity-based commitment for each of the three two-week sprints.

Jump to: Sprint 1 · Sprint 2 · Sprint 3

Sprint 1

Duration: Weeks 1–2 Sprint Goal: Users can sign up and manually log a workout end-to-end.

Team Capacity

5 people × 2 weeks, minus 1 dev on-call day and 1 team offsite half-day → Effective capacity: 34 story points (based on team's historical velocity of ~35/sprint)

Committed Stories

ID Story Points Owner
FT-1 Email sign-up 3 Dev A
FT-2 Onboarding tutorial 2 Design + Dev B
FT-3 Log workout type/duration 5 Dev A
FT-5 Edit/delete workout entry 3 Dev B

Committed points: 13 (intentionally conservative — first sprint, new team cadence, plus foundational architecture work not story-pointed: auth backend, DB schema, CI/CD pipeline setup, ~18 points of unstoried infra work)

Definition of Done

  • Code reviewed by 1 peer
  • Unit tests passing
  • QA sign-off on staging
  • No P1/P2 bugs open

Risks flagged at planning

  • Auth backend is a new pattern for the team — timeboxed to 3 days, fallback to simpler email-link auth if OAuth proves too slow (see Risk Register R-01).

Outcome

Sprint completed on schedule. All 4 committed stories delivered + infra foundation laid for Sprint 2. Velocity: 13 points (story-pointed) + infra work.

Sprint 2

Duration: Weeks 3–4 Sprint Goal: Users can passively track steps and build a streak.

Team Capacity

Full team available. Planned capacity: 21 story points (velocity trending up after Sprint 1's infra investment).

Committed Stories

ID Story Points Owner
FT-6 Automatic step tracking 8 Dev A
FT-7 Weekly step chart 5 Dev B + Design
FT-8 Daily streak logic 5 Dev A
FT-9 Push notification reminder 3 Dev B

Committed points: 21

Mid-Sprint Change: Scope Creep Incident

On Day 6, the Head of Product requested an additional feature — a "streak freeze" (let users protect a streak once per week) — for the beta launch, based on early user testing feedback.

PM decision process: 1. Assessed impact: new story estimated at 5 points, team had 3 days of slack remaining based on early burndown trend. 2. Did not silently absorb it — brought it to the sponsor with two options: (a) add it and accept schedule risk on FT-9, or (b) defer to Sprint 3. 3. Sponsor agreed to defer to Sprint 3 backlog (added as FT-10 follow-up), protecting the sprint goal.

This decision and its impact on the burndown are visualized in 06-sprint-burndown-chart.png.

Outcome

All 4 committed stories delivered on time. Sprint goal met. Streak-freeze feature correctly deferred rather than causing a late-sprint scramble.

Sprint 3

Duration: Weeks 5–6 Sprint Goal: Harden the app for release and ship the MVP to beta.

Team Capacity

Planned capacity: 19 story points (slightly reduced — 1 day reserved for release activities and beta onboarding coordination).

Committed Stories

ID Story Points Owner
FT-11 Crash reporting integration 3 Dev A
FT-12 Regression test suite 5 QA
FT-4 Workout notes field 2 Dev B
FT-10 7-day streak badge (deferred from Sprint 2) 3 Dev A + Design

Committed points: 13 (remaining capacity reserved for bug-fix buffer, standard practice for a release sprint)

Release Checklist (owned by PM)

  • [ ] App Store beta build submitted (TestFlight)
  • [ ] Crash reporting dashboard live and monitored
  • [ ] Beta invite emails scheduled with Community Manager
  • [ ] Rollback plan documented with Engineering Lead
  • [ ] Go/no-go meeting held 2 days before launch

Outcome

MVP shipped on Day 13 of the sprint (1 day ahead of the 6-week deadline). 0 critical bugs at launch. Beta invites sent to 500 users as planned.
